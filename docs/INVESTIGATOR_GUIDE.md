# Investigator Guide

## Where your work lives

**Case → Investigator Workbench.** Tasks, leads, witnesses and field notes
against the case record.

## What the platform gives you

**Case → Evidence Coverage** shows, for each charged count, which elements have
material in the record and which have none. The elements with none are where
investigation usually has most to do — but read the wording carefully: an empty
cell means nothing in the record uses language matching that element. It does
not mean the material does not exist.

**Case → Litigation Strategy** lists, for every theme the record raises, what
is commonly needed for that theme and is not on file. For a Fourth Amendment
theme that is the warrant and its affidavit, the return, and body-worn camera
covering the stop. For chain of custody it is the complete property log and
every transfer signature.

These are starting points drawn from the record, not a checklist the platform
believes is complete.

## Uploading what you gather

Evidence uploaded through the case appears in the inventory, is fingerprinted
with SHA-256 as stored, and has its text extracted so findings can cite it.

A file that could not be read is reported as such, with the reason, in the
action centre on the dashboard. It is stored but nothing in it is searchable
and no finding can cite it — that usually means requesting a readable copy.

## What to be careful about

Every passage the platform shows is quoted from a document. If a quotation
looks wrong, check the document: the quotation is what the file says, and the
file may be the problem.

Media is stored and measured for running time but not transcribed. Nothing
spoken in a recording is searchable. A recording that carries no audio track,
or whose audio is effectively silent, is reported as such with the measured
peak level, which is worth knowing before you spend an afternoon listening.

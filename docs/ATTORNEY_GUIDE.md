# Attorney Guide

## What this platform will and will not do

It organises the record: what is in it, what is missing, and where every
statement came from. It does not decide guilt, assess the merits of a motion,
recommend filing anything, or predict an outcome. Where the record does not
settle a question the answer is UNKNOWN, and you will see that word rather than
a confident guess.

Every passage shown is quoted from a document in your case, with the document
named, so you can check it against the original. If you cannot check it, do not
rely on it.

## Charges

**Case → Charges.** Current charges come from the most recent filing. Everything
filed before it stays exactly as filed, because that earlier pleading is the
basis of every motion already served on it.

**File a charging document** opens the workspace. Choose the document kind and
the California code, enter the section and the People's wording, tick the
allegations pleaded, preview, and file. The wording is stored verbatim and is
never paraphrased — it is what a motion quotes.

Pasting the text of a complaint reads its counts automatically. The confidence
of each field is shown and the result is a proposal for you to check, never a
filing. Anything you correct is marked so the parser will not write over it.

An amendment can be started from a prior filing, carrying counts and
allegations forward. A draft changes nothing until you file it.

Counts are matched across filings on the provision charged, not the count
number, so a count moving from 2 to 3 reads as renumbered rather than as a
dismissal and an addition.

## War room

**Case → War Room.** One screen: the stage the case is at and how that was
worked out, the operative charges, the issues the record raises with the
passage that raised each, what is not in the record, and what has changed.

The stage is inferred from what is on file. If you know better, set it — your
judgement is never overwritten by inference.

## Defence strategy

**Case → Litigation Strategy.** The record organised into 31 themes. A theme
appears because a document said something, and the passage is quoted.

A theme with nothing behind it is listed too, and says so. "Nothing found on
alibi" and "we never looked" are different answers, and you need to know which
one you are reading.

## Evidence coverage

**Case → Evidence Coverage.** For each count, the elements taken from the
statute itself with the material in the record beside them. Material that cuts
the other way is shown separately.

The most useful column is the empty one: an element with nothing behind it.
That may mean the material has not been produced, has not been processed, or
does not exist — the platform will not tell you which, because it does not know.

**Explain this** on any count answers why it is displayed, what produced it,
what supports and conflicts with it, and what is undetermined.

## Motions

**Case → Motions.** Issues the record raises, organised for your review. Each
says "this repository-backed issue may warrant attorney review" and nothing
stronger. The platform does not recommend filing and does not assess merits.

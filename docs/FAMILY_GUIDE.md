# Guide for Families

## What you will see

Sign in and you will find a summary of the case in plain English: where it
stands, what is charged, the next court date, what has recently been added, and
some questions you might want to ask the attorney.

## What this is not

It is not legal advice. It does not say whether anyone is guilty or innocent,
and it cannot tell you how the case will end. Only the attorney can advise you.

If something on the page worries you, that is a good reason to ring the
attorney — not a reason to draw a conclusion from it.

## Where the case is

The stage is explained in a sentence. "Discovery", for instance, means both
sides are exchanging the evidence they hold, and that stage often takes months.
A long stage is normal and is not a sign that something has gone wrong.

## The charges

These are shown in the words the prosecution used, not a summary written by
this platform. If the wording is hard to follow, that is because charging
documents are written that way. Ask the attorney what it means in practice.

## The questions

These come from the case file itself, not from a generic list. Each one says
why it is being raised and names the document that prompted it.

They are things worth asking about. They are not accusations that anything has
been missed — the attorney may already have all of it in hand, and often will.

## What you will not be able to see

Some material in a criminal case is restricted, and some is distressing. What
you can see depends on what the attorney has authorised. If you think you
should have access to something, ask them rather than this platform.
